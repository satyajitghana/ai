## The board, and why it is not sorted by CVSS

CVSS describes a bug's worst case in isolation. It does not say whether anyone is exploiting it or whether your copy is reachable. An unexploited 9.8 behind a VPN is a smaller problem this week than an 8.6 on an internet-facing gateway that CISA has watched being exploited. So the board ranks by three published facts:

- **Exploitation.** In CISA's KEV catalog scores 3. So does a malicious release that actually shipped, and a breach the operator itself has confirmed. A vendor's report of exploitation attempts, or keys that have been publicly recovered, scores 2. Nothing reported scores 0.
- **Access needed.** None scores 2. An authenticated user scores 1. An administrator or local access scores 0.
- **Reach.** An internet-facing appliance or web runtime scores 2. A bug that needs a specific configuration or data path scores 1. A local-only bug scores 0.

The total runs from 0 to 7. CVSS is shown, not added. Switch to "CVSS only" and watch GitLab's 10.0 jump to the top and LiteLLM, which has no score at all, fall to the bottom.

## eMudhra: eight CA keys anyone could recompute

### What happened

eMudhra is an Indian certificate authority whose emSign roots are trusted by the Chrome, Mozilla, Apple and Microsoft root programs. On 17 October at 18:05 a researcher who goes by Wayne emailed all four and eMudhra. He reported that the private keys of eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates were recoverable, with signed proof that he held all eight ([Mozilla Bugzilla 2083449](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2083449), reported).

The class of bug is simple to state. A P-384 private key is meant to be a random integer between 1 and a 384-bit group order. Mozilla's Dennis Jackson recovered the keys himself at 21:48 without the reporter's method, then wrote in the bug:

> The 8 impacted keys all used integers less than 256. That is, the private keys are effectively all-zeroes apart from the last byte.

The key space is about $2^{384}$; these keys came from 256 values. The curve and the signatures are fine. The keys were generated badly. Mozilla noted local AI agents recovered them on the first try. Several people recovered them independently, some publicly, which is why Mozilla escalated on 20 October (reported, comment 18). Scott Helme's [write-up](https://scotthelme.co.uk/eight-bits-of-trust-emudhras-ca-keys-cracked-in-milliseconds/) lists all eight values. One TLS intermediate's key was 4, and the two code-signing keys were 66 and 237, as the post said (reported, consistent with Mozilla's "less than 256"). Everything is revoked and blocklisted, so the numbers no longer open anything.

Which certificates: six TLS intermediates, `EM DV/OV/EV TLS CA - G3B` and `- G3C`, under `emSign Root TLS CA - G3`, and two code-signing intermediates trusted only by Microsoft. Sectigo had cross-signed the six under a root Apple and Microsoft still trust, and revoked the cross-signs on 19 October (comment 13). All eight came from a 30 April 2025 ceremony that made 24 intermediates; only the P-384 keys of the B and C series were weak (comment 5).

Apple's block on 19 October and Microsoft's on 20 October appear only in news reports. I could not find either vendor's own notice, so treat those two dates as reported second-hand.

### What is and is not affected

Google says no certificate chaining to the affected intermediates was in Certificate Transparency before the block, so no forgery could have worked against a CT-enforcing client (reported). eMudhra says seven of the eight were never in production and the eighth issued nothing ([status page](https://emudhra.com/en/post/certificate-security-incident), reported). That is not "no harm". A forgery needs no log entry to fool a client that skips CT and revocation checks, which is a lot of non-browser software. Cloudflare shipped fixed WARP, Cloudflare One and `cloudflared` (2026.10.2) builds for exactly that reason ([write-up](https://blog.cloudflare.com/emudhra-certificate-compromise/)).

The root removal is what breaks legitimate sites. eMudhra estimated "about 30,000 certs that require migration", 97% via ACME (comment 19). Any chain ending at `emSign ECC Root CA - G3` fails in Chrome once the 27 October removal lands. Google's instruction: "immediately acquire and install a new TLS certificate from another PKI hierarchy".

For India, MeitY said the issue is limited to eMudhra's global website-certificate business and does not touch the DSC and eSign services the Controller of Certifying Authorities regulates (reported, [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/impact-has-been-very-limited-meity-on-emudhra-security-incident-says-indian-dsc-and-esign-unaffected-562927-2026-10-21)). eMudhra agrees. I found no statement from the CCA itself. As far as I can tell, Indian DSCs chain to the CCA's own root, not to eMudhra's browser roots. If so, nobody filing returns with a USB-token DSC has anything to change (reasoned, not confirmed by the CCA).

### How to check

- **Browsers.** Nothing to do if they update normally. Restart Firefox for Android if it is older than 158.0.2. That release fixes profiles that were not applying the blocklist at startup (reported, comment 29).
- **Servers and services.** Update the OS CA bundle and any vendored ones, such as Python's `certifi`, Rust's `webpki-roots`, Java `cacerts` and container base images. Mozilla linked the `certifi` and `webpki-roots` updates in the bug. Node.js carries its own CA list. Its 21 October security release lists a `ca-cert` update for "eMudhra Intermediate CA Compromise" on 22, 24 and 26 (measured, from the release page). So the Node update below also fixes this.
- **Your own sites.** Check whether any certificate you serve chains to `emSign ECC Root CA - G3`. If one does, replace it before 27 October.
- **What not to do.** Cloudflare's line bears repeating: "It's important to not resolve these errors by disabling verification."

How keys made under an HSM key-generation policy ended up this weak is an open question on the bug. eMudhra's account, a faulty wrap and unwrap step in the ceremony script, is preliminary and unverified.

## PHP: php2shell, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619

### The leak, and the 10.0 that wasn't

Details leaked before the patch. watchTowr says information circulated on 20 October, ahead of the coordinated date, and that it confirmed the bugs were real (reported). PHP's notice that day reserved "the right to release earlier should details of either vulnerability become public". On 20 October a social post said PHP core had an RCE rated CVSS 10.0, with details due that day. PHP's own pre-announcement that day said "two critical vulnerabilities, both rated CVSS v4.0 9.2" and set the release for 21 October at 14:00 (reported, [php.net](https://www.php.net/)). The published CVE records agree with PHP. Both carry `CVSS:4.0/AV:N/AC:H/AT:P/PR:N/UI:N/VC:H/VI:H/VA:H/SC:N/SI:N/SA:N`, score 9.2 (measured). The "high complexity" and "attack requirements present" in that vector are what keep it under 10. **The CVSS 10.0 figure came from the leak, not from the advisory.**

### What they are

Both are memory-safety bugs in how PHP keeps state across the request lifecycle. Neither needs application code. In the CNA's words, from [GHSA-w8hw-w9fx-j79w](https://github.com/php/php-src/security/advisories/GHSA-w8hw-w9fx-j79w) and [GHSA-6wmf-4rv5-p3rp](https://github.com/php/php-src/security/advisories/GHSA-6wmf-4rv5-p3rp):

- **CVE-2026-109618, "RCE via OOM corruption".** An out-of-memory error can abort a request while internal request memory is only partly initialised. Request cleanup then treats that memory as valid.
- **CVE-2026-109619, "RCE via stale global state".** Request-owned global state can keep pointing at memory that has been freed. A later request on the same worker can use it before it is reset.

Both records list CWE-416 (use after free), CWE-755, CWE-824, CWE-1325 and CWE-1419 (measured). Both credit Wiz, Ryan at Calif.io and OpenAI as reporters. The fixes are credited to four PHP core developers. Wiz says it reported to PHP on 24 August (reported).

### Affected and fixed

| Branch | Fixed in | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 8.5 | 8.5.12 | supported |
| 8.4 | 8.4.27 | supported |
| 8.3 | 8.3.36 | supported |
| 8.2 | 8.2.35 | supported until 31 December 2026 |
| 8.1, 8.0, 7.4 | no release | minimal source backports from PHP; check your distro or LTS vendor |
| 7.3 and older | none | affected per watchTowr; upgrade |

The CVE records list only 8.2 through 8.5, each as "affected below the fixed version" (measured). PHP's news post adds the 7.4, 8.0 and 8.1 backports. watchTowr says older branches back to PHP 5 are affected too (reported). PHP 8.6.0 RC4 carries the fixes; earlier 8.6 builds do not.

PHP says every installation serving web requests is at risk, including PHP-FPM, Apache `mod_php`, `php-cgi`, FrankenPHP and LiteSpeed. It names `mod_php` the highest risk, "with a demonstrated exploit where an unauthenticated request can trigger the attack before any application code runs" (reported). The CLI is out of scope, since the bugs concern persistent workers serving many requests.

### Exploitation status

The sources disagree, so here they are separately:

- **CISA** had "not observed exploitation" on 21 October; its SSVC entry in both records says `Exploitation: none` (measured).
- **watchTowr** says its honeypots saw exploitation attempts against both CVEs from 21 October (reported). It does not say whether any succeeded.
- **A public exploit exists.** The "php2shell exploit" repository names both CVE ids; I am not linking it. Rapid7 found each public exploit worked only on a specific OS, PHP version and web server, and none looked broadly reliable (reported).
- **EPSS** on 22 October: 0.0576 for CVE-2026-109619 and 0.0531 for CVE-2026-109618, around the 93rd percentile (measured).
- **National CERTs** split too. The Dutch NCSC was reportedly investigating "first signs of successful misuse" on 22 October and HKCERT called both exploited; Bitdefender saw no attempts (all reported, via coverage).

So attempts are reported, not verified, and neither CVE was in KEV when I re-read the feed on 23 October (measured, catalog `2026.10.21`).

### The response: offline before patched

What made this week unusual was governments telling people to unplug before the fix existed:

- **CISA** (confirmed, [alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/21/critical-vulnerabilities-php-require-immediate-patching)): if you cannot update, or run 8.1 or older, take PHP-based systems offline until patched.
- **UK NCSC** (confirmed, [guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/news/critical-security-issue-affecting-php)): if you cannot update or mitigate, "you should consider taking the system offline until it can be fully mitigated", and look for backdoors before upgrading.
- **CERT-EU** (Security Advisory 2026-018), the **FBI** and the **NSA** gave similar advice on 21 October (reported, via coverage; I did not read their originals).
- **Japan.** Prime Minister Takaichi posted on X on the evening of 21 October: prepare to patch, and if you cannot patch quickly, consider suspending your site (reported, [Jiji via nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/yjj2026102101249/)). Coverage puts it at 21:55 JST, which is 12:55 UTC and 05:55 US Pacific. That is almost certainly the post's "5:55 in the morning" (reasoned).

Then the sites went dark:

- **Netherlands.** The NCSC took its own site offline, along with other central-government sites ([rijksoverheid.nl](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/10/21/aantal-websites-rijksoverheid-preventief-offline-na-kwetsbaarheden-php), confirmed). So did the Tweede Kamer ([NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/2026/10/21/dutch-parliament-website-taken-offline-cybersecurity-warning-triggers-precautions)). AFP adds the Port of Rotterdam, Overheid.nl, the KNVB and several hospitals (reported).
- **Elsewhere in Europe** (reported, AFP via [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261023/govts-companies-take-websites-offline-over-php-vulnerabilities)): Finland's Valtori, French prefecture sites, Germany's ITZBund, Belgian foreign-ministry sites and the Czech government site. Finland and Germany reported no exploitation.
- **Wikimedia** took some systems down while addressing the PHP vulnerabilities; MediaWiki is PHP. The **Internet Archive** went into emergency maintenance, PHP unnamed. **Something Awful's** maintenance page cites the flaw. In Japan, the Ministry of Finance, the Meteorological Agency and the National Tax Agency suspended services, and pixiv, DMM, niconico and BASE went into maintenance (all reported).

Nearly all were precautions, and most came back within a day. A day of downtime against unpatchable code on servers anyone could reach.

**Which applications.** The bugs sit below application code, so everything on a vulnerable PHP inherits them: WordPress, Drupal, Joomla, MediaWiki, Magento, Laravel (reasoned). Wiz demonstrated unauthenticated RCE against WordPress, Drupal and Joomla (reported). No source I found names Magento or Laravel; that changes nothing.

**How many servers.** I could not find the post's Censys figure of "about 1.5 million servers". CybelAngel counts 2.24 million internet-facing PHP hosts, 59% on branches with no fix coming, and calls it provisional (reported). Order of magnitude only.

### Mitigation, if you cannot update today

PHP is blunt about this: "There are no complete mitigations, updating is the only safe choice." Its stopgaps narrow specific paths. In PHP's own terms:

- **For CVE-2026-109618.** Set `memory_limit=-1` in a way applications cannot override, such as `php_admin_value` in the pool or the Apache config. Keep `post_max_size` low: 8M if you take no uploads, 32M if you do. Enforce request-size, field-count, multipart-part and nesting limits at the proxy. Use a container or cgroup memory limit, not `RLIMIT_AS` or `RLIMIT_DATA`, which PHP warns can cause the very allocation failure involved.
- **For CVE-2026-109619.** Set `session.upload_progress.enabled=0` in `php.ini` or the pool config and restart every worker. Setting it from `ini_set()` or `.user.ini` does not help. On 8.5, also set `fatal_error_backtraces=0`. Fix any fatal errors and timeouts an attacker can reach. Hiding them with `display_errors` or `@` does not count.

CISA's line for unsupported versions is harsher: take PHP 8.1-and-older systems that cannot be updated offline. Restart is the step people forget. A patched package on disk does nothing for an FPM pool that has been running since Tuesday.

### How to check

Check the version that is actually running (`php -v`, or `phpinfo()` behind authentication) against the table. For a distro package, look for the two CVE ids in its changelog. A backport keeps the old version number, which is why the checker below says "check your vendor" rather than "vulnerable" for a distro build. For detection, the indicators PHP worker processes produce are generic. CISA suggests watching for PHP workers that start shells or download tools. Wiz suggests watching for unusual multipart requests followed by memory-limit errors or repeated HTTP 500s, unusually large or malformed `PHPSESSID` cookies, and unexplained worker churn (reported).

## LiteLLM 1.107.0: a poisoned release, the second this year

This should top a Python shop's list. LiteLLM puts many model providers behind one API, so its proxy usually holds every provider key an organisation has. The maintainers' [incident report](https://docs.litellm.ai/blog/security-incident-oct-21-2026) is the source below unless marked.

- **What shipped.** `litellm==1.107.0` on PyPI, seven wheels, 11:39 to about 13:23 on 21 October. Docker Hub's `v1.107.0`, `latest`, `main-latest` and `main-stable` tags, 11:46 to 14:20. The same tags on GHCR for `litellm`, `litellm-database` and `litellm-non_root`, until about 13:51. The GitHub source tag is clean (reported).
- **What it does.** A credential stealer plus a loader that keeps fetching remote code. The maintainers say Python startup re-runs the planted files, so nothing had to import `litellm`: installing it and running any Python in that environment was enough (reported). Analysts describe it collecting SSH keys, cloud and CI credentials, and the provider keys in a LiteLLM proxy database ([StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/litellm-1-107-0-compromised-pypi-backdoored-wheels-credential-stealer), [Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/litellm-compromise), reported).
- **How it got out.** Per the maintainers, an attacker with stolen developer credentials overrode branch protections, pointed a release workflow at a lookalike repository, `Berri-AI/litellm`, and satisfied the approval gate with another account's credentials. They believe the credentials came from GitHub CLI OAuth tokens exposed in an internal deployment's logs, and say that is not yet confirmed (reported). No CVE has been assigned.
- **Second time this year.** In March, `litellm` 1.82.7 and 1.82.8 on PyPI carried credential-harvesting malware ([GHSA-5mg7-485q-xm76](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/GHSA-5mg7-485q-xm76), confirmed). The post's "second time this year" holds.
- **Who is exposed.** Anyone who installed 1.107.0 from PyPI, or pulled one of those image tags, in those windows. Unpinned installs and anything that tracks `latest` are the likely victims. The maintainers say enterprise customers on the private registry were not affected.

**Indicators the maintainers publish:** `cook.pth`, `cook.py` and `sitecustomize.py` in `site-packages`, matching `__pycache__/cook*.pyc` and `sitecustomize*.pyc` files, a `litellm-1.107.0.dist-info` directory, the lock file `/tmp/remote_code_loader.lock`, and the image digest `sha256:1bbcb1443bc962e45f5aad53fe3d2c30600e0379715fb6c664cdd3ac126c339e`. They warn that not finding these files does not prove a host is clean.

**What to do,** in the maintainers' order:

1. Do not run `pip install` first in an affected environment, because Python startup re-runs the planted files. Remove them, and stop the detached Python process holding the lock file.
2. Rebuild environments and images from scratch, pinned to `litellm==1.106.2` or another release from before the incident.
3. If 1.107.0 ran on a host with outbound internet access, assume its data left.
4. Rotate every secret that host could reach: the instance or task IAM role, every provider key in the proxy database, the master and virtual keys, the `DATABASE_URL` password, and anything else in the process environment.
5. On AWS, look in CloudTrail for `GetCallerIdentity` from the instance role around install time, and for that role's credentials being used from outside your infrastructure.

Pin by version and by digest. A tag called `latest` is a promise someone else keeps.

## Three hosting platforms, three breaches

Three platforms disclosed breaches within a week. Railway ties its incident to a series of attacks on platform companies aimed at AI inference providers. That is Railway's framing; I do not link the three beyond it.

- **Railway** (confirmed, [disclosure](https://blog.railway.com/p/security-disclosure-wednesday-october-21st), 21 October). Railway detected anomalous activity in the systems that store customer environment variables. An unauthorized party may have accessed encrypted values and, for some, the decrypted contents. Initial analysis suggests a subset of users, but Railway tells every user to rotate all environment variables, API keys and private registry credentials, and to review service logs. All deployments were paused during the investigation. Railway says it asked upstream providers to revoke keys proactively. Status: `status.railway.com`.
- **TiDB Cloud** (confirmed, PingCAP's [incident report](https://www.pingcap.com/trust-hub/security/security-update-october-2026/), 19 October). Someone used stolen service-account credentials to reach internal systems from 9 September to 7 October, copied data and obtained source code. PingCAP says the exported records include account names and emails, SQL editor text, billing and contract data, and a table of *encrypted* TiDB Cloud API-key values. It found "no evidence that data stored in TiDB was impacted". It invalidated all identified credentials by 14 October. That is "about a month" of access, as the post says. The **10.6 TB** figure is an extortion group's claim. PingCAP neither confirms nor mentions it, so it is unverified.
- **Zeabur** (confirmed, [Zeabur's post](https://zeabur.com/blogs/zeabur-is-shutting-down), 17 October). An attacker reached internal systems and some customer services on 14 October. Zeabur says the incident was broader than its late-August one. So "second breach in two months" holds. The company is shutting down: shared clusters pause on 1 December 2026 and everything stops by 31 December. BleepingComputer reports more than 3.2 million environment-variable records and 321,810 accounts affected (reported; Zeabur's post does not give counts).

On any of these, the response is the same: rotate every credential that lived in the platform, at the provider that issued it.

## GitLab: a CVSS 10.0 in every self-managed release since 7.0

- **What it is.** [CVE-2026-110264](https://docs.gitlab.com/releases/patches/patch-release-gitlab-19-5-1-released/), an integer overflow when compiling a specially crafted regular expression. Under certain conditions it could let an unauthenticated user run code on the GitLab server (confirmed, CVE record). CWE: integer overflow.
- **Severity.** `CVSS:3.1/AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:H`, **10.0** (measured). EPSS on 22 October: 0.00505 (measured).
- **Affected and fixed.** GitLab CE/EE from 7.0, fixed in 19.5.1, 19.4.3, 19.3.6, 19.2.9, 19.1.9, 19.0.10 and 18.11.13, released 21 October (measured). GitLab 7.0 shipped in 2014, so the post's "since 2014, every self-hosted GitLab for twelve years" is right (reasoned). GitLab.com and GitLab Dedicated are already patched.
- **Not the same as last week's.** GitLab fixed a different CVSS 10.0 regex bug, CVE-2026-97229, on 12 October, in 19.2 to 19.4 only. A server patched then, say to 19.4.2, is still affected by this one (measured, from both records).
- **Exploitation.** None reported by GitLab. Not in KEV (measured). The reporter is credited through HackerOne.
- **Fix and check.** GitLab lists no workaround, so upgrade. Single-node instances take downtime for migrations. GitLab publishes two detections for self-managed instances: crashes of the native regex worker, and bursts of render errors (reported). Check the version on the Admin area's dashboard or `/help`.

## WordPress "XSS2Shell": CVE-2026-64638 is August's bug with a new exploit

A post on 22 October announced "XSS2Shell", a pre-auth XSS-to-RCE chain in WordPress, with a public PoC repository I am not linking. The advisory side:

- **Component.** WordPress **core**, not a plugin: a pre-auth reflected XSS on the login screen (confirmed, [CVE record](https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2026-64638)).
- **Severity.** `CVSS:4.0/AV:N/AC:H/AT:N/PR:N/UI:A/VI:H/...`, **8.9** (measured). Reaching code execution needs a victim to visit an attacker's site and interact: the record says "successful social engineering of and explicit interaction by the target victim".
- **Fixed** in [WordPress 7.0.3](https://wordpress.org/news/2026/08/wordpress-7-0-3-release/), released 6 August 2026, and backported to every branch back to 4.7 (confirmed). Credited to pwn.ai.
- **Exploitation.** CISA's SSVC says none. EPSS 0.0089 on 22 October (measured). Not in KEV.

So this is not new: a two-month-old fix got a public exploit. Sites with background updates were patched in August; check that yours took it.

## Node.js: Node2Shell, CVE-2026-111497, and two TLS bypasses

- **What it is.** An out-of-bounds write (CWE-787) in the Brotli decoder of Google's Brotli 1.0.3 through 1.2.0, which Node.js bundles. So it is a core runtime bug, not an application pattern. The Node.js [October 2026 security release](https://nodejs.org/en/blog/vulnerability/october-2026-security-releases) rates it high and credits OpenAI Codex Security.
- **Severity.** Google, as CNA, scores it `CVSS:4.0/AV:N/AC:L/AT:P/PR:N/...`, 9.2 (measured). No EPSS score yet on 22 October.
- **Fixed** in 22.23.4, 24.22.1 and 26.12.1 (21 October). End-of-life lines, 20 and older plus 23 and 25, get nothing. Standalone Brotli: 1.0.10, 1.1.1, 1.2.1.
- **Who is exposed.** Code that decodes untrusted Brotli. Imperva's [write-up](https://www.imperva.com/blog/node2shell-from-brotli-data-to-remote-code-execution/) found two paths: built-in `fetch()` reading a Brotli response, and Express 5 body parsers decoding Brotli bodies. A bare `http` server does not decode on its own (reported).
- **Exploitation.** Imperva reports unreliable code execution on the official 24.22.0 Linux arm64 build; a public PoC exists. Nothing in the wild, not in KEV, SSVC `none` (measured).
- **TLS bypasses, same release** (measured, from the release page). Two **high**: a certificate hostname bypass "when the IP is written fullwidth digits" (CVE-2026-105483), and one "when the host loosely resolves to an IP address" (CVE-2026-106069). One medium, CVE-2026-106072, lets DNS SANs vouch for IP endpoints. All three are mismatches between what the client thinks it reached and what the certificate covers.
- **Fix and check.** Update the runtime everywhere, including Node binaries inside container images, which package inventories miss. WAF rules from Imperva and Vercel cover inbound requests only, not a Brotli response you fetch. The same release carries the eMudhra CA update.
