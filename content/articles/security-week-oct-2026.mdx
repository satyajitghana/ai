This is a write-up of the past week's exploits: the eMudhra issue, the PHP RCEs, and whatever else carried a CVSS 10 or was being exploited. It is meant for defenders. For each item I give the vulnerability class, the affected and fixed versions, the severity as published, whether it is exploited, who is exposed, and how to check. I do not cover how any of these bugs are exploited. Where a public exploit exists I say so and nothing more.

I worked from primary records. The CVE records came from the CVE Services API (`cveawg.mitre.org`). The Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list came from CISA's JSON feed, catalog version `2026.10.21`. EPSS scores came from FIRST's API, dated 22 October. The rest came from vendor advisories and the Mozilla bug where the eMudhra incident is being worked. Every number below is labelled **measured** (I read it from one of those files), **reported** (a vendor or researcher says so, and I could not re-check it), or **reasoned** (my own arithmetic on the other two). All times are UTC.

## The board, and why it is not sorted by CVSS

CVSS describes the worst case of a bug in isolation. It does not tell you whether anyone is exploiting the bug, or whether your copy is reachable. A 9.8 behind a VPN with no exploitation is a smaller problem this week than an 8.6 on an internet-facing gateway that CISA has watched being exploited. So the board ranks by three facts that are published for every item:

- **Exploitation.** In CISA's KEV catalog scores 3. A vendor's report of exploitation attempts, or keys that have been publicly recovered, scores 2. Nothing reported scores 0.
- **Access needed.** None scores 2. An authenticated user scores 1. An administrator or local access scores 0.
- **Reach.** An internet-facing appliance or web runtime scores 2. A bug that needs a specific configuration or data path scores 1. A local-only bug scores 0.

The total runs from 0 to 7. CVSS is shown but not added in. Switch the sort to "CVSS only" and watch Cisco's 9.8 and Node's 9.2 move up, and Ivanti's 8.6 move down. Then decide which order you would patch in.

## eMudhra: eight CA keys anyone could recompute

### What happened

eMudhra is an Indian certificate authority. Its emSign roots are trusted by the Chrome, Mozilla, Apple and Microsoft root programs. On 17 October at 18:05 a researcher who goes by Wayne emailed the four programs and eMudhra. His report said the private keys of eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates could be recovered, and it included signed proof that he controlled all eight keys. That timeline is his, posted to [Mozilla Bugzilla 2083449](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2083449) (reported).

The class of bug is simple to state. A P-384 private key is meant to be a random integer between 1 and a 384-bit group order. Mozilla's Dennis Jackson recovered the keys himself at 21:48 without the reporter's method, then wrote in the bug:

> The 8 impacted keys all used integers less than 256. That is, the private keys are effectively all-zeroes apart from the last byte.

The key space is about $2^{384}$, and these eight keys were drawn from 256 values. Nothing about the curve or the signatures is broken. The keys were simply generated badly. Mozilla noted that local AI agents also recovered them on the first try (reported). Several people recovered them independently and some posted that publicly, which is why Mozilla escalated on 20 October (reported, comment 18).

Which certificates: six TLS intermediates, `EM DV/OV/EV TLS CA - G3B` and `- G3C`, all issued by `emSign Root TLS CA - G3`, and two code-signing intermediates trusted only by Microsoft. Sectigo had cross-signed the six TLS intermediates under a root that Chrome and Mozilla no longer trust but Apple and Microsoft still do. Sectigo revoked those cross-signs on 19 October (reported, comment 13). All eight came from one key ceremony on 30 April 2025, a batch of 24 intermediates. Only the P-384 keys in the B and C series were weak (reported, comment 5).

| 27 Oct (PDT, planned) | Root removed from the Chrome Root Store on all devices | Google |

Apple's block on 19 October and Microsoft's on 20 October appear only in news reports. I could not find either vendor's own notice, so treat those two dates as reported second-hand.

### What is and is not affected

Google says no TLS certificate chaining to the affected intermediates had been logged to Certificate Transparency before the block. So no forged certificate could have worked against a client that enforces CT (reported). eMudhra says seven of the eight intermediates were never used in production, and that the eighth had issued nothing (reported, [status page](https://emudhra.com/en/post/certificate-security-incident)). Those claims are not the same thing as "no harm". A forged certificate does not need to be logged to work against a client that skips CT and revocation checks, and that describes a lot of non-browser software. Cloudflare's [write-up](https://blog.cloudflare.com/emudhra-certificate-compromise/) is useful here: it shipped fixed WARP, Cloudflare One and `cloudflared` (2026.10.2) builds because those clients carry their own trust stores.

The root removal is the part that breaks legitimate sites. eMudhra estimated "about 30,000 certs that require migration", 97% of them issued through ACME (reported, comment 19). Any site whose chain ends at `emSign ECC Root CA - G3` will fail in Chrome once the 27 October removal lands. Google's instruction to those operators is to "immediately acquire and install a new TLS certificate from another PKI hierarchy".

For India: the Ministry of Electronics and IT said the issue is limited to eMudhra's global website-certificate business. It said the issue does not affect the Digital Signature Certificates and eSign services regulated by the Controller of Certifying Authorities (reported, [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/impact-has-been-very-limited-meity-on-emudhra-security-incident-says-indian-dsc-and-esign-unaffected-562927-2026-10-21)). eMudhra says the same. I found no statement from the CCA itself. As far as I can tell, Indian DSCs chain to the CCA's own root rather than to eMudhra's browser-trusted roots. If that holds, someone filing returns with a USB-token DSC has nothing to change because of this incident (reasoned, not confirmed by the CCA).

### How to check

- **Browsers.** Nothing to do if they update normally. Restart Firefox for Android if it is older than 158.0.2. That release fixes profiles that were not applying the blocklist at startup (reported, comment 29).
- **Servers and services.** Update the OS CA bundle and any vendored ones, such as Python's `certifi`, Rust's `webpki-roots`, Java `cacerts` and container base images. Mozilla linked the `certifi` and `webpki-roots` updates in the bug.
- **Your own sites.** Check whether any certificate you serve chains to `emSign ECC Root CA - G3`. If one does, replace it before 27 October.
- **What not to do.** Cloudflare's line bears repeating: "It's important to not resolve these errors by disabling verification."

A separate question on the bug asks how keys made under a policy of HSM key generation ended up this weak. As of 23 October eMudhra had not answered it. eMudhra's account of a faulty wrap and unwrap step in the ceremony script is preliminary and unverified.

## PHP: php2shell, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619

### The leak, and the 10.0 that wasn't

On 20 October a social post said PHP core had an RCE rated CVSS 10.0, with details due that day. PHP's own pre-announcement that day said "two critical vulnerabilities, both rated CVSS v4.0 9.2" and set the release for 21 October at 14:00 (reported, [php.net](https://www.php.net/)). The published CVE records agree with PHP. Both carry `CVSS:4.0/AV:N/AC:H/AT:P/PR:N/UI:N/VC:H/VI:H/VA:H/SC:N/SI:N/SA:N`, score 9.2 (measured). The "high complexity" and "attack requirements present" in that vector are what keep it under 10. **The CVSS 10.0 figure came from the leak, not from the advisory.**

### What they are

Both are memory-safety bugs in how PHP keeps state across the request lifecycle. Neither needs application code. In the CNA's words, from [GHSA-w8hw-w9fx-j79w](https://github.com/php/php-src/security/advisories/GHSA-w8hw-w9fx-j79w) and [GHSA-6wmf-4rv5-p3rp](https://github.com/php/php-src/security/advisories/GHSA-6wmf-4rv5-p3rp):

- **CVE-2026-109618, "RCE via OOM corruption".** An out-of-memory error can abort a request while internal request memory is only partly initialised. Request cleanup then treats that memory as valid.
- **CVE-2026-109619, "RCE via stale global state".** Request-owned global state can keep pointing at memory that has been freed. A later request on the same worker can use it before it is reset.

Both records list CWE-416 (use after free), CWE-755, CWE-824, CWE-1325 and CWE-1419 (measured). Both credit Wiz, Ryan at Calif.io and OpenAI as reporters. The fixes are credited to four PHP core developers. Wiz says it reported to PHP on 24 August (reported).

### Affected and fixed

| Branch | Fixed in | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 8.5 | 8.5.12 | supported |
| 8.4 | 8.4.27 | supported |
| 8.3 | 8.3.36 | supported |
| 8.2 | 8.2.35 | supported until 31 December 2026 |
| 8.1, 8.0, 7.4 | no release | minimal source backports from PHP; check your distro or LTS vendor |
| 7.3 and older | none | affected per watchTowr; upgrade |

The CVE records list only 8.2 through 8.5, each as "affected below the fixed version" (measured). PHP's news post adds the 7.4, 8.0 and 8.1 backports. watchTowr says older branches back to PHP 5 are affected too (reported). PHP 8.6.0 RC4 carries the fixes; earlier 8.6 builds do not.

PHP says every installation serving web requests is at risk, including PHP-FPM, Apache `mod_php`, `php-cgi`, FrankenPHP and LiteSpeed. It names `mod_php` the highest risk, "with a demonstrated exploit where an unauthenticated request can trigger the attack before any application code runs" (reported). The CLI is out of scope, since the bugs concern persistent workers serving many requests.

### Exploitation status

The sources disagree, so here they are separately:

- **CISA**, in its alert of 21 October, had "not observed exploitation". Its SSVC entry in both CVE records, timestamped 21 October, says `Exploitation: none` (measured). Neither CVE is in KEV as of catalog `2026.10.21` (measured).
- **watchTowr** says its honeypots saw exploitation attempts against both CVEs from 21 October (reported). It does not say whether any succeeded.
- **A public exploit exists.** The repository behind the "php2shell exploit" repost names both CVE ids. I am not linking it. Rapid7 reviewed the public exploits and found that each worked only on a specific mix of OS, PHP version and web server, and that none looked broadly reliable (reported).
- **EPSS** on 22 October: 0.0576 for CVE-2026-109619 and 0.0531 for CVE-2026-109618. Both sit around the 93rd percentile (measured).

So exploitation is confirmed as attempted by one vendor and unconfirmed by CISA. The attempts are reported, not verified.

### Mitigation, if you cannot update today

PHP is blunt about this: "There are no complete mitigations, updating is the only safe choice." Its stopgaps narrow specific paths. In PHP's own terms:

- **For CVE-2026-109618.** Set `memory_limit=-1` in a way applications cannot override, such as `php_admin_value` in the pool or the Apache config. Keep `post_max_size` low: 8M if you take no uploads, 32M if you do. Enforce request-size, field-count, multipart-part and nesting limits at the proxy. Use a container or cgroup memory limit, not `RLIMIT_AS` or `RLIMIT_DATA`, which PHP warns can cause the very allocation failure involved.
- **For CVE-2026-109619.** Set `session.upload_progress.enabled=0` in `php.ini` or the pool config and restart every worker. Setting it from `ini_set()` or `.user.ini` does not help. On 8.5, also set `fatal_error_backtraces=0`. Fix any fatal errors and timeouts an attacker can reach. Hiding them with `display_errors` or `@` does not count.

CISA's line for unsupported versions is harsher: take PHP 8.1-and-older systems that cannot be updated offline. Restart is the step people forget. A patched package on disk does nothing for an FPM pool that has been running since Tuesday.

### How to check

Check the version that is actually running (`php -v`, or `phpinfo()` behind authentication) against the table. For a distro package, look for the two CVE ids in its changelog. A backport keeps the old version number, which is why the checker below says "check your vendor" rather than "vulnerable" for a distro build. For detection, the indicators PHP worker processes produce are generic. CISA suggests watching for PHP workers that start shells or download tools. Wiz suggests watching for unusual multipart requests followed by memory-limit errors or repeated HTTP 500s, unusually large or malformed `PHPSESSID` cookies, and unexplained worker churn (reported).

## Node.js: Node2Shell, CVE-2026-111497

The Hacker News covered "Node2Shell" on 23 October. The primary sources exist, so this is more than a news item.

- **What it is.** An out-of-bounds write (CWE-787) in the Brotli decoder (`c/dec/decode.c`) of Google's Brotli library 1.0.3 through 1.2.0. That library is bundled into Node.js core, so this is a core runtime bug, not an application pattern. The Node.js [October 2026 security release](https://nodejs.org/en/blog/vulnerability/october-2026-security-releases) lists it as "Node.js Brotli decoder out-of-bounds write permits remote code execution" and rates it high. It credits OpenAI Codex Security for the report.
- **Severity.** The CVE record, filed by Google as CNA, scores it `CVSS:4.0/AV:N/AC:L/AT:P/PR:N/UI:N/VC:H/VI:H/VA:H/SC:N/SI:N/SA:N`, 9.2 (measured). Node.js's own rating is high. No EPSS score was published for it on 22 October (measured).
- **Affected and fixed.** Lines 22, 24 and 26 are affected, fixed in 22.23.4, 24.22.1 and 26.12.1, released 21 October. End-of-life lines (20 and older, plus 23 and 25) get no fix. Standalone Brotli is fixed in 1.0.10, 1.1.1 and 1.2.1.
- **Who is exposed.** Only code that decodes untrusted Brotli data. Imperva's [write-up](https://www.imperva.com/blog/node2shell-from-brotli-data-to-remote-code-execution/) found two exposure paths: the built-in `fetch()` reading a Brotli-compressed response, and Express 5 body parsers that decode Brotli request bodies. A bare `http` server that receives a Brotli body does not decode it by itself (reported).
- **Exploitation.** Imperva reports code execution on the official Node.js 24.22.0 Linux arm64 build in its test setups. It took several attempts and did not always succeed. A public proof of concept exists. No in-the-wild exploitation has been reported, and the CVE is not in KEV (measured). CISA's SSVC says `Exploitation: none` (measured).
- **Mitigation and check.** Update the runtime, including Node binaries bundled in container images, which a package inventory can miss. Imperva and Vercel added WAF rules for inbound requests. Those rules do nothing for a Brotli response your service fetches from someone else's server. Only the update covers that path.

## The KEV additions, 13-20 October

CISA's catalog added 15 CVEs between 13 and 20 October, 13 of them from 14 to 20 October (measured). Most have a remediation due date three days after they were added under BOD 26-04. A few, including two of the Plex bugs and the Siemens bug, have 14 days (measured). Each entry below gives the class, the CVSS as the CNA published it, and the vendor's fix. The KEV entries do not say which threat actors are involved, so neither do I.

**Unauthenticated, internet-facing (patch first):**

- **Ivanti Connect Secure, CVE-2026-104935.** Added 13 Oct. Authentication bypass by spoofing that lets a remote attacker run commands. CVSS 3.1: 8.6. EPSS 0.260 (measured). The CVE text says 22.7R2.14 and earlier are not affected.
- **Cisco Secure Email Gateway, CVE-2026-76519.** Added 14 Oct. Path traversal in the AMP file-reputation handling of attachments, leading to arbitrary file write. CVSS 3.1: 9.8. Advisory `cisco-sa-esa-afw-zqYJkjxp`.
- **Ahsay AhsayCBS, CVE-2026-105133 and CVE-2026-105134.** Added 14 Oct. An authentication bypass that chains with OS command injection, affecting up to 10.3.2. The CNA (VulDB) scores the injection 10.0 under v3.1 and v4.0. This is the one **CVSS 10** in the week's exploited set (measured). EPSS 0.383, the highest on this list (measured). The CVE vectors mark a proof of concept as existing.
- **Atlassian Data Center products, CVE-2026-21589.** Added 15 Oct. Unauthenticated read of files in the web root across Bitbucket, Confluence, Jira, Jira Service Management, Bamboo, Crowd, Crucible and Fisheye. CVSS 4.0: 9.3. EPSS 0.127. Fixed versions are per product in Atlassian's advisory.
- **BeyondTrust Remote Support, CVE-2026-102764.** Added 16 Oct. Improper origin validation in internal messaging components, allowing file operations on the appliance. CVSS 4.0: 9.3. Fixed in 26.2.3 (advisory BT26-05).

**Authenticated or conditional:**

- **Plex Media Server, CVE-2026-96652, -96654, -96655 and -96656.** Added 20 Oct. Two SSRFs and a URL injection that CISA says can chain into an admin-only file write. All four are fixed in 1.43.3.10861. It matters for home labs that expose Plex for remote streaming.
- **Windows Shell, CVE-2026-96951.** Added 13 Oct. Command injection leading to a security feature bypass. CVSS 3.1: 8.8, user interaction required. Fixed in the October cumulative update.
- **Palo Alto PAN-OS, CVE-2026-0322.** Added 14 Oct. Code injection in the management interface, as root, by an authenticated admin. CVSS 4.0: 8.6. The vendor's mitigation is the same as ever: do not expose the management interface.
- **Fortinet FortiOS, FortiProxy, FortiPAM and FortiSwitchManager, CVE-2025-61624.** Added 16 Oct. Path traversal through admin CLI commands. CVSS 3.1: 5.4. Advisory FG-IR-26-122. An old CVE newly confirmed as exploited.

**Local:**

- **SiteGuard Server Edition, CVE-2026-106931.** Added 20 Oct. OS command injection that runs as root or SYSTEM. Local, CVSS 4.0: 8.5.
- **Siemens Software Center, CVE-2026-110936.** Added 20 Oct. Uncontrolled DLL search path. Local, needs authentication, fixed in V4.0.9.8.

## The AI stack: a quiet week

I looked for critical bugs in inference servers, agent frameworks and MCP servers disclosed in the window and found none. The one relevant CVE is **vLLM CVE-2026-114049**, published 21 October. It is a cross-request leak in which one client's token-allowlist mask can be applied to another client's request. That is an integrity problem, not code execution: CVSS 4.0 6.3, CVSS 3.1 3.7. It is fixed in vLLM 0.31.0 (measured). It is worth patching on a shared endpoint and worth knowing about if you offer constrained decoding to several tenants.

The AI story this week is a different one. OpenAI is credited on both PHP CVEs, and OpenAI Codex Security on the Node.js one. eMudhra's keys were reportedly first broken with AI-assisted tooling, and Mozilla says local agents recovered them on the first try from a one-line prompt. One week is a data point, not a trend, but I would budget for more like it. Related reading here: sandboxing agents that run code in [Perplexity SPACE](/articles/perplexity-space) and [NVX](/articles/nvx), and what a vendor's own disclosure can establish in [ZCode, open-sourced](/articles/zcode-disclosure).

## What I could not verify

- Apple's and Microsoft's own notices for their eMudhra blocks. I have only news reports for the dates.
- The root cause of the weak eMudhra keys. eMudhra's script-flaw account is preliminary, and the questions on the bug about HSM generation are open.
- Whether any watchTowr-observed attempt against PHP succeeded. Also the claim that PHP 5 is affected, which comes from watchTowr only.
- Cisco's and Ivanti's per-branch fixed versions. I did not retrieve their advisories, so I give the advisory ids rather than version numbers. BeyondTrust's advisory page returned 403 here, so its fixed version (26.2.3) comes from the CVE record.
- The October Oracle Critical Patch Update, which was scheduled for 20 October. I found no primary or secondary account of its contents, so it is left out rather than guessed at.

## What to do on Monday

1. Inventory PHP everywhere, including inside appliances and images. Update to the branch's fixed release and **restart the workers**. If you cannot, apply PHP's stopgaps and restrict exposure.
2. Patch the five unauthenticated KEV items if you run them, Ivanti and Ahsay first by EPSS. Look for compromise rather than assuming you weren't hit: they were being exploited before you heard about them.
3. Update CA bundles in services and images. Check that nothing you serve chains to `emSign ECC Root CA - G3` before 27 October.
4. Update Node.js to 22.23.4, 24.22.1 or 26.12.1 in every image, prioritising services that fetch or decode Brotli from untrusted parties.
5. Patch Plex if it is reachable from the internet, and vLLM if it serves several tenants.
